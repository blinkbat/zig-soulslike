# The giant shield-knights — DS1 Anor Londo Sentinels (+ lineage) (reference)

PURE Dark Souls 1 / Demon's Souls reference, `ELDEN_RING.md`'s rule: real mechanics, real numbers,
nothing about this game. Compiled from Fextralife / wikidot / Fandom souls wikis and community threads.

## The SENTINEL (Anor Londo)

Giant brass knights — they "resemble enormous stone statues" — wielding the **Giant's Halberd** (right
hand) and the **Giant Shield** (left), in a dark variant of the Giant Set. Statue-still until provoked;
most "will not attack you unprovoked" and their aggro leashes are tiny. Cannot be parried, cannot be
backstabbed. HP 535 (NG) / 937 (NG+); souls 1,500. Often posted in PAIRS.

**The whole moveset is FIVE things** (damage figures are the Fandom table's):

| Attack | Dmg | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Horizontal sweep | 350 | right-to-left arc, "covers a good bit of ground"; near-**360°** — "can strike players even behind them" |
| Double sweep | 350×2 | the second sweep is same-direction and **"slightly delayed… will chop you down if you roll too early"** — the authored roll-catch |
| Overhead slam | 560 | "a bit of a wind up", "very poor tracking" once launched — easier dodged than blocked |
| Thrust | 420 | "longest range of them all, **only used when no other attacks will reach**"; "may be followed up with a quick horizontal swing" |
| Shield slam | 460 | "long build up time, mega damage and a small AoE"; **"leaves a huge opening"** — the designated punish, and the best moment to roll through the legs |

**The guard is a wall.** "Their shield will fully block every damage type. They're basically impossible
to kill when attacking from the front if they're not in an attack animation." In the crouched stance
(hunkered behind the planted shield) they are "completely invulnerable from the front." The shield
"functions more like a wall than a shield" — the ways in are BENEATH it (ankles — their critical spot is
"anything lower than knee") and AROUND it. Blocking THEM drains huge stamina.

**Movement.** "These guys are slow. Really slow." They walk, never run, and "turn slowly and can be
rolled through" — but they are "**surprisingly quick to respond to your movements, often jumping out of
the way**": the liveliness is a discrete **sideways hop** (one big weight shift), not turn speed. The
canonical fight rhythm, in one player's words: "You lure, you wait him for attack, block/dodge, flank
him, hit twice, ready for his attack again."

**Royal Sentinels** (the pair before Ornstein & Smough; 755 HP, blue tabards, larger): add **Wrath of
the Gods** (AoE shove when hugged) and **Great Heal** at critical HP — a full stop, exposed "for almost
3 seconds."

## BERENIKE KNIGHT (DS1) — the human-scale tower-shield wall

"Great, hulking hollowed soldiers": mace/greatsword + **Tower Shield**. Shield bash, heavy horizontals,
chained verticals, a charge; "slow to move or turn", "most powerful attacks are forward facing";
parryable but not riposte-able, not backstabbable. Heals with Estus below 40 % — interrupt it. Punish
beat documented: "wait 1/2 of a second or so after his strike, then land a few blows."

## TOWER KNIGHT (Demon's Souls) — the ancestor

A **30-foot** knight (player "just below its knee"), tower shield + lance, 1,365 HP. **Spear stab** (used
on grounded players still far away — the range answer), **Shield Slam** ("extremely slow and
well-telegraphed but massive area — hitting even a few paces BEHIND him — and very high damage"),
vertical shield slam, boot stomp (fast, weak, tiny area — the proximity tax). Leg damage topples him
backward ("also something to stay away from") for a head-punish window.

## The lineage's one design

An oversized knight whose **shield is a wall, not a parry tool**; whose damage window lives **beneath or
around the shield-line, never through it**; whose attacks are **few, enormous, fully committed, with
roll-catch timing built into the follow-ups** (the delayed second sweep, the thrust's quick-swing chain,
the rear-reaching slam); and whose threat is defused by **position, not reflexes**. Passivity + a tiny
leash is what makes them read as architecture that wakes up.
